vCard QR Code (Digital Business Card)

One scan saves your full contact record &mdash; name, role, company, phone, email and website &mdash; with no typing and no app.

Last updated: October 2026 · Free · no account · no watermark

What it does: encodes a vCard, the standard contact format. Scanning offers to add you to the phone's address book, so your details land in Contacts rather than being forgotten on a card.

A paper business card gets thrown away. A vCard QR code gets saved, because the friction between meeting someone and having them in your phone drops to a single tap.

It uses vCard, an open standard supported by every modern phone. There is no app to install on either side. The record is written into the code itself, so it works even if your website is down.

How to create a vCard QR code

  1. Choose the vCard type Open Make My QR Code and select vCard.
  2. Fill in your details Name, organisation, job title, phone numbers, email, website and address. Only include what you want strangers to have.
  3. Keep it lean Every extra field makes the pattern denser. A name, one phone, one email and a website scan far more reliably than a full record.
  4. Download the PNG Print it on the back of your card, a name badge or a sticker.
  5. Test on both platforms Scan with an iPhone and an Android phone. Save the contact, then delete it after testing.

  • Freelancer and contractor invoices

Best practices

  • Put the code on the back of a card so the front stays clean and branded.
  • Add one line of instruction next to it: &ldquo;Scan to save my contact&rdquo;. Most people will not scan an unexplained square.
  • Avoid including a photo &mdash; it increases the payload enormously and is not supported consistently.
  • Re-generate the code whenever your number or role changes. It is static, so it cannot be updated.

Common mistakes with vCard codes

vCard is an old, loosely implemented standard. Most problems come from asking the format to carry more than a phone can display cleanly.

  • Encoding everything. Multiple phone numbers, two addresses, a fax and a photo make the pattern dense and the saved record messy. A name, one number, one email and a website is enough.
  • Forgetting the country code. Store numbers in international format so an overseas contact can actually call you back.
  • No instruction text. An unexplained square gets ignored. Print "Scan to save my contact" next to it.
  • Stale details. The record is frozen at print time. If you change role or number, the old cards keep handing out the old information.
  • Assuming every phone behaves the same. iOS and Android present the save prompt differently, and some scanners mis-map multiple numbers.

Printing and sizing

On a business card, a 2 cm code on the reverse is normally sufficient, provided the contrast is strong and the card stock is not glossy. On a badge or a sticker read at arm's length, allow 3 cm.

Keep the code away from folds and the card edge. Because vCard payloads are longer than a simple URL, the modules are smaller for a given physical size — so if in doubt, go one size larger than you would for a link code, and test it on a mid-range Android phone rather than the newest iPhone in the office.

Frequently asked questions

Does it save the contact automatically?

No. The phone shows a preview and asks for confirmation before adding the person to Contacts.

Why do some fields appear mixed up on certain phones?

Not every scanner implements the vCard standard identically, particularly for multiple phone numbers and addresses. Keeping the record simple avoids almost all of these problems.

Can I update the vCard later?

No. The data is inside the code, so changes require a new code and a new print run.

Is a vCard QR code better than a link to my website?

They solve different problems. A vCard puts you in someone's address book; a link QR code takes them to a page. Many people print both.

Why does the saved contact show the wrong fields?

Some scanner apps implement vCard inconsistently, especially with multiple phone numbers and addresses. Reducing the record to one number, one email and one website resolves nearly every case.

Can I put a vCard code in an email signature?

Yes, and it works well there because the reader is already at a screen. Keep the image large enough to scan — around 150–200 pixels square — and link the image to your website as well, for people reading on the same device.
